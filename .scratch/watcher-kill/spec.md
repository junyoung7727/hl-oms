## Problem Statement

Since U19a the watcher reads the journal with its own reader, queries Bybit with its own key, runs W1–W4, holds plain orders on a confirmed difference (`watcher_mismatch`) and sends heartbeats. It cannot yet act when the OMS itself is gone. If the OMS process dies or stops answering HTTP, its resting orders stay at Bybit and nobody cancels them: the OMS's own kill loop (C-KILL) runs inside the OMS, and Bybit offers no venue-side dead-man for this account (`docs/contracts/kill.md`, DCP is for institutional clients). Nor does anyone hear of it: the watcher sends no message, and the observability stack is a separate path that may be down too.

U19b adds, inside the `watcher` crate and still sharing no code with the OMS:

1. its own Telegram sender with the PR #3 delivery rules and the prefix `[WATCH P1]`, for a confirmed mismatch, a rejected key (retCode 10010, 10003, 33004 and the rest of the never-retried class), a public IP outside the key's list, and every kill notice;
2. its own cancel-all confirmation loop with its own key (C2–C5 of table 7-8: cancel-all, count the open orders with `/v5/order/realtime`, repeat every 1 s, watch 30 s after the first zero), with the P1 rule of C4 and a position block in every notice;
3. W5: when `/api/status` gives no good answer for 10 s, the watcher writes the kill marker, stops the OMS (`watcher_kill` on `admin.sock`; if that fails, `systemctl stop` of the OMS unit, the only unit it may stop), and only then runs the loop.

## Common decisions (all parts)

### Independence
- Still no dependency on any `oms-*`, `md-*` or other workspace crate (`alert-direct` included); dev `http-fake` only. No new registry crate: the sender uses `reqwest` (already there, with its `resolve()` for the pinned route), the stopper uses `std::process::Command` and `Child::try_wait`, the marker write `tokio::task::spawn_blocking` (feature `rt`, already there); no new tokio feature. `Cargo.lock` changes only by one line in U19b3b: `oms-node` in the dependency list of `watcher-conformance` (dev, the conformance test against the OMS's marker reader; review 2 N4); the `watcher` crate's own dependencies do not change. - The texts of the P1 notices are written anew in the watcher (the OMS's are a model only). Where the watcher's rule differs from the OMS's on purpose, the part says so and C-WATCH part 11 says so too. ### Clock and waits
- Every timer of U19b (W5 probes and silence, the loop's rounds, watch, notices and order budget, the stop wait, the mismatch notice pace, the repeat rule, the sender's flush pace) reads the injected `Clock`. Nothing sleeps on it: each sans-IO part returns the instant it next wants to run, the runtime's 100 ms tick (`runtime.rs:38`) asks again. The sender's network timeouts (10 s request, 3 s connect) and the client's `request_timeout_ms` are tokio time, as in U19a; each sans-IO request and each kill step (K1 marker, K2 halt, K3 stop) also has a deadline on the injected clock so a lost answer, a lost task or a hung file write never blocks a machine (review 2 S1). At its deadline the runtime aborts the task of a loop request, a position read or a K2 halt (`AbortHandle`; review 2 S2): nothing of an abandoned request runs beside the next one. An answer or a page that the sans-IO part handles at `now ≥` its deadline is past the deadline (count error, `Failed`, `포지션 조회 실패(시간 초과)`), also when it and the passed deadline reach the loop in the same turn with the answer first (review 3 S2); the two exceptions are a K2 or owed-halt reply and a W5 probe answer, which are taken when they arrive (the truth about the OMS first). A task that sends several pages (the count, the position read) reads the injected clock after each page and sends no further page when that page answered at or after its deadline (review 3 S2): the abort is for a request that hangs, not the only stop. A K1 write runs on `tokio::task::spawn_blocking` (in the `rt` feature already) and cannot be aborted; its late outcome is ignored. The runtime is single-threaded (`runtime.rs:219-233`, `Builder::new_current_thread`, `main.rs:34`), so nothing of U19b does file or process I/O inline in the tick, with one exception: the stopper's `Command::spawn`, `Child::try_wait`, `Child::kill` and the non-blocking read of its stderr after exit (K3 below; none of them waits; review 3 S6).
- Where an injected-clock deadline bounds real I/O (review 4 S2): K1 (the marker write and its two `sync_all`), K2 and the halt retries (`admin.sock`), K3 (`systemctl`), the position read, and every request of the loop (`Inflight`). In production the injected clock is wall time, so the deadline is real time. In a test on the manual clock it is not: a test that moves the clock while such a step runs gives the step less real time than its deadline says (`walk` gives K1's 2,000 ms about 300 ms of real time), and the outcome then depends on the machine. So manual-clock tests do not walk through these steps: the rig waits for open requests before it moves the clock (Test rig, U19b2c), and the runtime tests stop the clock at the kill steps (`walk_kill`, Testing Decisions).
- A task that runs beside the tick (the sender task, U19b1b) never sleeps on the injected clock either: it waits on a `tokio::sync::Notify`; `Handle::once`/`set` notify it, and the runtime's tick notifies it when the sender's `next_flush(now)` on the injected clock is due (review 1 N6). So manual-clock tests move it with the clock, not with real time.
- A clock that steps back holds the timers (`saturating_sub`), as U19a, with one exception for the kill path (review 4 S3): `SystemClock` is wall time (`clock.rs:14-23`), so a backward step of Δ would hold W5 (no probe and no silence judged until the clock passes the old value) and a running kill (the next cancel-all at `t_r + round_ms`, the K1–K3 and request deadlines, the progress marks) for Δ, and nothing else cancels for an OMS that is gone. So U19b3a adds `clock::Steady`, a `Clock` wrapper: it returns the inner clock plus an offset, and when the inner clock reads lower than the last value it returned it raises the offset so the value it returns stays at that last value (a step back becomes a pause of zero length; a step forward passes through and W5's gap rule handles it); the offset and the last value sit in one `Mutex` so the tasks that read it (count reader, position reader) see one timeline. The runtime (U19b3c) gives `Steady` to W5, `Kill` (and its `KillLoop`), `Inflight`, the count reader, the position reader and the K-step deadlines; the raw clock stays for everything else (request signing, every KST or UTC text, the engine, the sender's flush pace, `Repeat`, `MismatchNotices`). Texts that name an instant of the kill path are written from the raw clock: the header's `마지막 정상 응답` is raw `now − silent_ms`, the marker text's UTC the same. Known limit (C-WATCH part 12): after a backward step the sender's retry pace and the repeat rule wait up to Δ (a new message still goes at once, its `Notify` wakes the sender). Time that the watcher did not observe (its own process stopped or starved, or a clock step forward) is not OMS silence: W5's gap rule below (review 2 S3). Known limit in C-WATCH part 12: a real OMS silence that overlaps such a gap is counted from the end of the gap, so W5 fires up to one gap later.

## U19b2 decisions: the cancel-all confirmation loop (U19b2a: POST, key rules, position block and texts; U19b2b: the loop)

### POST and cancel-all (`watcher::sign`, `watcher::rest`; U19b2a)
- `sign::post_prehash(ts, api_key, recv_window, body)` = `ts + api_key + recv_window + body`, the same HMAC as GET. Test vectors: the published POST string to sign (facts Q1.10, exact bytes) and HMAC values for a made-up secret computed with Python's `hmac` for this part (allow-listed in `tools/ci/secret_scan_allow.txt` with the reason, as U19a3 did).
- `Client::cancel_all() -> Result<CancelAll, RestError>`: the only signed request that is not a GET, and the only POST to the Bybit REST base. (The watcher has two other POSTs, both unsigned and to other hosts: the VM import push, `metrics.rs:141`, since U19a3, and Telegram `sendMessage`, `alert.rs`, U19b1b; review 1 M1.) There is no generic `post(path, body)`: the path `/v5/order/cancel-all` and the body bytes `{"category":"linear","settleCoin":"USDT"}` are constants in `rest.rs`, the signed string and the sent body are the same bytes, `Content-Type: application/json`, the five headers as GET. It goes through the guard, the 403 pause and the key latch like every request (`rest.rs:397-425`), and counts in `watcher_requests_total{endpoint="/v5/order/cancel-all"}`. So no code path of the watcher can create or amend an order (the watcher key needs the `Order` permission to cancel, which would also allow creating). Test `client::the_only_post_is_cancel_all_with_the_fixed_body` (also: the URL is the REST base plus the path constant, and the signed and sent bytes are the body constant). A source test `client::no_other_post_exists` reads `crates/watcher/src/*.rs` at test time (plain text, a comment counts too, as the independence scan) and asserts: `.post(` appears exactly once in each of `rest.rs` (in `cancel_all`), `metrics.rs` (VM import) and `alert.rs` (Telegram) and in no other file; `Method::`, `.request(`, `.put(`, `.patch(` and `.delete(` appear in no file (so no request can hide behind a generic method). `.get(` is not checked (map lookups use it); every signed GET goes through `Client::get`.
- Answer: the client classes of U19a (`rest.rs:184-215`). retCode 0 with `result.success` absent or `"1"` → `CancelAll { cancelled: result.list.len() }`; `result.success == "0"` → `refused` ("cancel-all answered success 0"); `result.list` not an array → `unreadable`.
- The kill scope is linear/USDT: every configured symbol settles in USDT (start step 5 since U19a4b); orders in other settle coins are not seen by this loop or by W1 (C-WATCH part 11 says so, as table 7-8 text does).

### Start rule for a key that must cancel (`watcher::start`, step 2; U19b2a)
When `[kill]` is configured (U19b2a adds the section with its loop fields; U19b3a makes it required on mainnet), query-api must also show: `readOnly == 0` (integer), `permissions.ContractTrade` a list containing `"Order"`, and no `"Withdraw"` in any list of `permissions`. Otherwise the start is refused (exit 2) with the rule named. Without `[kill]` these are not read (a read-only key is then fine). The `Order` → cancel-all link is inference from the permission names (Q4), not documented: testnet experiment (C-WATCH part 11 lists it under "확인 안 됨"). `Withdraw` is master-only per Q4, so a sub-account key should never carry it; refusing it costs nothing (design §7.4 "출금 권한 없음"; ops doc line 87 said the start does not check it, U19b2 changes that line). The recheck (`IpCheck`) applies the same rules; a failure there is a rule stop (`stop_key`), as U19a4b's other rules (owner question 5).
- Amendment (U19b2a review 1 S4): a rule that reads and fails refuses even when another field or list of the same answer does not read (`readOnly` an integer ≠ 0; `ContractTrade` read without `Order`, or absent; `Withdraw` in any list that reads); the answer is unreadable (start: ask again after 5 s; recheck: a failed recheck, ask again after `ip_check_ms`) only when no readable rule fails.

### The loop (`watcher::kill::KillLoop`, sans-IO; U19b2b)
`KillLoop::new(timing, request_timeout_ms)`; inputs `start(now, header, outcome: StopOutcome)` (the final notice's last line, below; U19b2a defines the type, U19b3b gives the value), `cancel_done(now, req_id, CancelAnswer)`, `count_page(now, req_id)` (one page of the count answered; review 2 S2), `count_done(now, req_id, Result<usize, String>)`, `set_outcome(StopOutcome)` (a halt retried during the loop latched, U19b3b; review 3 M1), `poll(now)`, `abort(now, why)`; output actions `CancelAll {req_id}`, `Count {req_id}`, `Notice(Notice)`, `Ended(Report)`; accessors `phase()`, `round()`, `remaining()`, `cancel_all_calls()`, `next_wake()`. `CancelAnswer` = `Done(CancelAll)` | `Failed(why)` (sent, and no usable answer or refused) | `NotSent(NotSent)` with `NotSent` = `Latched` (the key latch, `auth_stop()` is `Some`) | `Paused` (the 403 pause) | `Local(why)` (a local error): nothing left the host. The loop keeps the last `NotSent` kind for the progress text (review 2 S5).

| State | Entered | Does | Next |
|---|---|---|---|
| C2 cancel | round `r` starts at `t_r` | `CancelAll` if the order budget admits it, else waits for the budget's instant | `Done` or `Failed` → C3 at once; `NotSent` → C4 |
| C3 count | after a sent cancel-all | `Count`: `/v5/order/realtime?category=linear&settleCoin=USDT&limit=50`, every page by W1's paging rule (`w1.rs:77-118` `read`, the cap at `:110-115`, `MAX_PAGES` = 20 at `:30`; more → count error "more than 1000 open orders"; review 3 N8), `n` = rows with `orderStatus ∈ {New, PartiallyFilled, Untriggered}`, any `orderLinkId` (foreign orders count) | `n = 0` → C5; `n > 0` → C4; error → C4 (a failed count is never zero) |
| C4 repeat | `n > 0` or a failure | waits until `t_r + round_ms` | C2, round `r + 1` |
| C5 watch | first `n = 0` of this pass at `z` (every entry into C5 takes a new `z`: after a count error or `n > 0` in the watch the loop goes through C4/C2/C3 again and the watch starts over; review 4 N6) | `Count` at `z + k × watch_every_ms`, the last at `z + watch_ms` | `n > 0` → C2 at once (round `r + 1`); count error → C4; the count due at `z + watch_ms` answered 0 → End (however late it answered; a slow answer to an earlier count is not it, as the OMS's N4) |
| End | — | final notice, `Ended(Report {rounds, cancel_all_calls, zero_since})` | idle |
| Aborted | `abort` (shutdown or a start refusal during the loop, U19b3d) | one abort notice, `Ended` with `aborted` | idle |

Never two requests of the loop in flight (review 1 N7): a `Count` or `CancelAll` that is due while one is open waits for its answer or its deadline, then goes at once (so a watch count due at `z + k × watch_every_ms` may go late; the count that ends the watch is the first one emitted at or after `z + watch_ms`), as the OMS's loop.

- **Deliberate difference from the OMS (C2 failure)**: the OMS goes from a failed cancel-all to C4 without a count (`kill.rs:238-259`). The watcher counts after every cancel-all that was sent, also a failed one. The count is the truth (zero open orders is the goal, whoever cancelled them), and the answer of cancel-all when nothing is open is not documented (Q11.7): if Bybit refused a cancel-all with nothing to cancel, a loop that never counts after a refusal would never end. A cancel-all that was not sent (latch, pause) is not followed by a count (the count would not be sent either).
- **Order budget**: at most 2 `CancelAll` in any half-open window `[t, t + 1,000)` ms, counted from emission (design: 2 per second for the emergency path, table 7-8 C4). With config-valid timings (`round_ms ≥ 1,000`, `watch_every_ms ≥ 1,000`) two cancel-alls are never closer than 1,000 ms (the next comes at `t_r + round_ms`, or on the watch path at least `watch_every_ms` after `z ≥ t_r`), so the budget never binds there (review 1 S2). It is a guard against a later change of the timings or the paths, not a rule the valid config ever meets. A cancel-all the budget holds back is emitted at the budget's instant (from `poll`). `KillLoop::new` takes the timings directly (the config ranges are checked at config load only), so the budget test builds it with `round_ms = 100`, `watch_every_ms = 100` (below the ranges), where the budget does bind, and mutant (17) is killed there.
- **Deadlines** (review 2 S2): a `CancelAll` has the deadline emission + `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 ms (the engine's `DEADLINE_EXTRA_MS`, sized for one request: the guard's 10 s window plus 1 s, `engine.rs:54-57`). A `Count` is up to 20 requests, so its deadline is per page: emission + `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 ms, and each `count_page(now, req_id)` with `now` before the deadline in force moves it to `now + request_timeout_ms + 11,000` (an 11-page count at 1.3 s per page is not failed while its pages answer). A page, a `count_done` or a `cancel_done` handled at `now ≥` the deadline in force is past it (review 3 S2): the count is a count error, the cancel-all `Failed`, the deadline is not moved; the same holds when `poll` sees the deadline first. Past a deadline, `poll` takes it as `Failed("no answer before the deadline")` / count error; a later answer or page with that `req_id` is ignored (one debug line).
- **Abort at the deadline** (review 2 S2): `kill::Inflight` (U19b2c, production code used by the rig and by the runtime of U19b3c) spawns the client call of each `CancelAll` and `Count` as a tokio task, keeps its `AbortHandle` and the deadline the loop gave, and at every step aborts the task whose deadline passed, before the loop emits the next request. A count task sends each page and its result over the loop's channel, and keeps the same per-page deadline itself on the injected clock (emission, then each page's answer, + `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 ms): after a page that answered at or after its deadline it sends no further page and ends (review 3 S2; a count task asks for page k+1 at once after page k, with no await the runtime could use to abort it in between, `rest.rs:416-417`). So an abandoned count sends no further page, also by one request, never two requests of the loop run at once, and the guard's 60 per 10 s is taken only by live requests. U19b2c builds the count reader and `Inflight`; U19b2b's sans-IO loop is tested without them.
- **Bound of the loop**: none in rounds (it runs until zero is confirmed or the process stops), as the OMS's loop and table 7-8. Requests per round ≤ 1 cancel-all + 20 count pages (an aborted task sends no more); the request guard (60 per 10 s) is the hard bound; at 500 open orders a round needs 11 count pages and the guard stretches rounds (as U19a9's budget note; the engine is paused during a kill, U19b3).
- Amendment (U19b2b review 1 S3): `start` acts only on a loop that never started; a second `start` (a request open, in the watch, after the end or after an abort) returns no action and changes nothing (one kill, one loop; U19b3b/c call `start` from the kill sequence). Test `kill::a_second_start_does_nothing`.

### Notices (the C4 rule) and their text (rule U19b2b; `position_block`, `notice_text` U19b2a)
- Kinds: **start** (at `start`), **progress** (from `poll`: progress notice `k` is due at `s + k × notice_ms`, `s` the instant of the start notice, and is sent at the first `poll` at or after its mark while the loop is not ended; a late notice does not move the next mark, and two or more marks passed at one `poll` give one notice, the next mark then the first one after `now`; review 3 S3), **final** (at End), **abort** (at `abort`). Anchored marks make the bound below exact however late the runtime polls (the runtime polls at its tick, 100 ms of real time, `runtime.rs:220-227`, which a test on a fast manual clock turns into seconds of manual time). So after the start one progress notice per `notice_ms` while the loop runs, with the latest round, count (known or `모름: <why>`) and error.
- **Deliberate difference from the OMS (notices; review 1 S4)**: the OMS sends a change notice at once when a known count above zero differs from the last shown, plus a repeat every 60 s (`kill.rs:192-235`, kind `Change`); TN `05_trading.html:213` says "60초마다, 그리고 남은 건수가 바뀔 때 보냄". The watcher sends no change notice: a changed count waits for the next progress notice. Reason: the scope's "at most once per 60 s" and the 12-in-10-min bound, which the OMS's change notices do not keep (each count change is one more message). So with both loops running after `watcher_kill`, `[OMS P1] 킬 루프` may show a count change up to 60 s before `[WATCH P1] 워처 킬`. C-WATCH part 11 states it as the second difference. Owner question 2: the scope's "only when the remaining count changes and at most once per 60 s" and table 7-8's "바뀌지 않아도 60초마다 한 번" are read together as "at most once per 60 s, also when the count did not change": a person learns every minute that orders are still open or the loop still fails. A changed count is shown at the next notice, not at once (the scope's "at most once per 60 s").
- Bound: in any window of length T the start and progress notices are ≤ 1 + ⌈T / notice_ms⌉ (each notice after the first one in the window covers a mark of its own inside the window, because one notice covers every mark passed before it; a window that holds the start notice holds ≤ ⌊T / notice_ms⌋ marks after it), + 1 final or 1 abort: with a 10-minute query failure 1 + 10 + 1 = 12 (design R2 SHOULD 4). The final notice is a separate kind and is never held back by `notice_ms`. The kill sequence adds at most one `LateHalt` notice per kill (review 3 M1), outside the loop's 12; known limits count it.
- A notice is text plus a position block read just before it is sent (U19b3c runtime; here: `kill::position_block(rows: Result<&[Value], &str>, kst) -> String` and `kill::notice_text(&Notice, header) -> String`, pure).
- Position block: from `GET /v5/position/list?category=linear&settleCoin=USDT&limit=200` and cursor pages (at most 5, 판단(모델 아님); the reader keeps its deadline, `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 ms from the read's start (review 4 N2), on the injected clock and sends no further page after one that answered at or after it, review 3 S2); `settleCoin` alone returns only rows of size > 0 (Q12.1); rows with `size` 0 or unreadable are left out. Per row: `- {symbol} {매수|매도} {size}, 미실현손익 {unrealisedPnl}, 마크 {markPrice}, 마크에서 강제청산가까지 {d} %`, `d = |mark − liq| / mark × 100` rounded down to 0.01 % with the watcher's `Dec` arithmetic: `Dec` has only `parse`, `checked_add`, `checked_sub`, `checked_neg`, `is_zero` (`decimal.rs:34-80`, an `i128` scaled by 10^8), so U19b2a adds `Dec::percent_floor(num, den) -> Option<Dec>` (`num ≥ 0`, `den > 0`: `num.0 × 10^4 / den.0` in checked `i128`, integer division toward zero, the result read as hundredths of a per cent; any overflow → `None` → `모름(계산 넘침)`), with its own unit tests (review 3 N3); `liqPrice` empty, zero or unreadable, or `markPrice` ≤ 0 or unreadable → `모름(거래소가 청산가를 주지 않음)` / `모름(마크 가격 없음)`. Head line `포지션(거래소 /v5/position/list, {KST}, 강제청산가는 교차 증거금이면 거래소 추정):`. No row → `포지션 없음`. The query fails (any client class) → `포지션 조회 실패({class}). 수량·손익·마크·강제청산가 모름`. The client is latched or paused → no request, `포지션 조회 안 함({why}). 수량·손익 모름`. At most 20 rows are listed, then `그 밖에 {m}종목` (판단(모델 아님): keeps one notice within one 3,500-unit part, so "12 P1" stays 12 Telegram messages).
- Amendment (U19b2a review 1 S3): only rows of `size` 0 are left out; a row whose `size` does not read is a position of unknown size and is shown with `수량 모름` in the `{size}` place, so the block never says `포지션 없음` for it.
- Texts (body; the runtime adds the header of U19b3):
  - start: `워처 킬 시작({header}). 워처 키로 미체결 0건 확인까지 {round_ms/1000}초마다 일괄 취소(linear, USDT 정산). 포지션은 닫지 않음.`
  - progress: `워처 킬 {r}회차: 남은 미체결 {n}건(또는 모름: {why}), 일괄 취소 {k}회. {last_error}`; when the last `CancelAnswer` was `NotSent(Latched)` the last error is the fixed line `워처 키가 멈춰 일괄 취소를 보낼 수 없음: 워처를 다시 시작해야 루프가 끝남` (review 2 S5), and for `NotSent(Paused)` `워처 요청 일시 정지(HTTP 403): 정지가 풀리면 다시 보냄`.
  - final: `워처 킬 정리 완료: 미체결 0건 확인 뒤 {watch_ms/1000}초 동안 늦은 접수 없음({r}회차, 일괄 취소 {k}회). ` then one line by `StopOutcome` (review 2 M1; the sequence of U19b3b sets it):
    - `Latched` (K2 halted): `OMS는 래치됨(watcher_kill). 해제는 운영자만: omsctl clear-hold --reason kill --note …`
    - `StoppedMarked` (K3 `stopped`, the marker written or already present, and its path checked against the OMS's in this process, `marker_checked`): `OMS는 멈춤(systemctl stop). 다음 시작은 킬 마커로 래치됨. 해제는 운영자만: omsctl clear-hold --reason kill --note …`
    - `StoppedUncheckedMarker` (K3 `stopped`, the marker written or already present, but `marker_checked == false`; review 3 S1): `OMS는 멈춤(systemctl stop). 킬 마커는 OMS가 읽는 경로인지 확인 못 함: 워처는 OMS가 다시 답하면 watcher_kill을 보냄`
    - `StoppedUnmarked` (K3 `stopped`, the marker not written): `OMS는 멈춤(systemctl stop). 킬 마커가 없어 다음 시작은 래치 없이 시작함: 워처는 OMS가 다시 답하면 watcher_kill을 보냄`
    - `Unconfirmed` (K2 not halted, K3 failed or unconfirmed): `OMS 정지 미확인: OMS가 살아 있으면 래치 없이 주문을 낼 수 있음. 마커는 다음 시작에만 걸림. 워처는 OMS가 다시 답하면 watcher_kill을 보냄`
    So the final notice never says the OMS is latched when nothing latched it. A halt retried during the loop that latched (U19b3b, review 3 M1) makes the outcome `Latched` before the final notice.
  - abort: `{워처 종료 | 워처 시작 거부: <reason cut to 200>}로 워처 킬 중단({r}회차, 남은 미체결 {n | 모름}, 일괄 취소 {k}회). OMS의 래치·마커는 그대로.` No position read (the watcher is ending), and the notice ends with the fixed line `포지션 조회 안 함(워처 종료 중). 수량·손익·마크·강제청산가 모름`, so it is still "shown as unknown" (review 1 M2); `kill::notice_text` writes that line for `Abort`, the runtime adds no block.
- The OMS's notices say `킬 루프`; the watcher's say `워처 킬`, so a person can tell the two loops apart in one chat (both run when `watcher_kill` latched the OMS).

### Test rig (U19b2c)
`crates/watcher/tests/common/kill_rig.rs`: the manual clock, a real `Client` against an `http-fake` with scripted routes for `/v5/order/cancel-all`, `/v5/order/realtime`, `/v5/position/list`, and a step loop: `poll(now)`, start each action through `kill::Inflight` (the production executor, so the rig runs the abort rule the runtime runs), feed the pages and answers that arrived, move the clock to `min(next_wake, now + 100)`. A route may move the manual clock by a scripted amount when it answers (the `on_request` hook), so a slow venue is simulated on the manual clock. Every loop has a step bound (판단(모델 아님): 20,000 steps); the rig fails the test when it is reached. `http-fake`'s `on_request` hook records the manual clock at each request, so the order-rate assertions use the loop's clock, not real time.
- When the rig may move the clock (review 4 S1): `Inflight` runs each request as a real tokio task against `http-fake`, which takes real time (milliseconds) to answer, while the loop's `next_wake` with a request open is its deadline (13 s away). A step loop that does not wait would pass that deadline in manual time before the answer arrives. So before a step moves the clock, the rig waits in real time (inside the 5,000 ms net below) until every open request has delivered to the loop what it will deliver: a cancel-all its `cancel_done`, a count its `count_done` (all its pages), a position read its result. The one exception is a request whose route the test scripted as `Answer::Hang`: the rig's `on_request` hook marks the request as hanging when it arrives, and the rig then moves the clock without waiting for it (so `kill::a_count_past_its_deadline_sends_no_further_page` can pass the deadline while the page hangs). Only then does the clock move to `min(next_wake, now + 100)`. A route that moves the clock at its answer (`on_request`) does so inside the request, before the answer, so the loop sees that answer at the moved instant. This rule is why the recorded cancel-all instants are on the loop's clock and the same on every machine: no rig test's outcome depends on how fast the fake answers.
- Guard hang (review 1 S3): `Client::turn` reads the injected clock but waits in real time until the guard admits (`rest.rs:397-425`); the rig moves the clock only between steps, so a 61st request in 10 s of manual time would wait forever. The rig wraps every client call in a real-time `tokio::time::timeout` of 5,000 ms (판단(모델 아님)) that fails the test with `the guard held a request: the rig's clock did not move`. Every rig test stays under 60 requests in any 10 s of manual time, and says its count in a comment: e.g. `kill::more_than_1000_open_orders_is_a_count_error` runs two rounds (2 × 21 = 42 requests) and stops; the 600 s failure tests send ≤ 1 cancel-all + 1 count per 1,000 ms round (≤ 20 per 10 s). The 5 s timeout is only the net under a wrong count.

Otherwise exit 2: `"the OMS reads its kill marker at <path or none>; the watcher writes <path>: they must be the same file"`. A marker the OMS never reads would not keep a killed OMS halted at its next start. The field is read from the status only, the OMS config is not read (independence).

### W5 (`watcher::w5::W5`, sans-IO, U19b3a)

What W5 sees (review 1 S1): `/api/status` is built from the published state under a read lock (`oms-node/src/status.rs:43-46`), not by the core loop. So W5 fires when the OMS process is gone or no longer answers HTTP; a core or pipeline that hangs while the HTTP task still answers is not seen by W5 (the heartbeat's missing `recorded` only changes `watcher_heartbeat_ok_ts`). The design names `/api/status` as the signal (§7.5 W5), so the rule stays; the limit is written in C-WATCH part 12 and V-WATCH-13 `not_covered`, and owner question 11 asks whether a heartbeat without `recorded` should also count.

States: `Off` (no `[kill]`), `Unarmed`, `Armed {last_good_ms}`, `Killing`, `Rearming {halt_owed}`.
- `arm(now)`: `Unarmed → Armed {last_good_ms: now}`. The runtime arms W5 when the start machine returns `Done` or `StayUpNoRequests` (after `auth`: the loop then cannot send, and its notices say so, but the marker and the stop still work). Before that the account is not verified, and a cancel-all must never go to an unverified account (as the OMS, `.scratch/kill-switch/spec.md` "Not armed before the boot checks pass"). Owner question 1: W5 is armed whether or not the OMS ever answered in this process, so a watcher started while the OMS is down kills 10 s after its start checks.
- `poll(now) -> Option<Probe {req_id}>`: in `Armed` and `Rearming`, one probe every `probe_ms` from the previous probe's emission, never two in flight; a probe without an answer at emission + `request_timeout_ms` + 1,000 ms (판단(모델 아님)) is a failed probe. No probe in `Killing`.
- Gap rule (review 2 S3): `poll` remembers the instant of the previous poll. When two polls are more than `2 × probe_ms + request_timeout_ms` apart (판단(모델 아님); 4,000 ms with the defaults, against the 100 ms tick), the time between them was not observed by the watcher (its process stopped or starved, a VM pause, a clock step forward): `last_good_ms` and the deadline of the probe in flight move forward by the gap, and one `warn` line names the gap. Unobserved time is not OMS silence.
- `on_answer(now, req_id, good: bool)`: good = `Status::get` gave 2xx JSON with a 16-hex `core.instance_id` (the boot rule without the cap, `runtime.rs:105-116`). `Armed`: a good answer sets `last_good_ms = now`. `Rearming {halt_owed: false}`: a good answer → `Armed {now}`; `Rearming {halt_owed: true}`: see the owed halt below. A failed or bad answer changes nothing (except that it counts as a failed probe for `fire`). An answer to a probe that is not in flight is ignored.
- `fire(now) -> Option<Fire {last_good_ms, silent_ms}>`: in `Armed`, when `now − last_good_ms ≥ silence_ms` and at least one probe emitted at or after `last_good_ms` has failed (review 3 N1: a probe emitted in the tick of the good answer has emission `== last_good_ms`) (an answer that is not good, or its deadline) → `Killing`, once (review 2 S3). The second condition never delays a real kill: a probe emitted at `last_good_ms + probe_ms` fails by `last_good_ms + probe_ms + request_timeout_ms + 1,000 ≤ last_good_ms + silence_ms` (the `silence_ms` bound above). The probe is W5's own (the engine's status requests do not count), so W5 is one rule that a test can drive.
- `kill_ended(halt_owed: bool)`: `Killing → Rearming {halt_owed}`: after a kill, W5 waits for one good answer before it watches again, so an OMS that stays down (stopped by the watcher) does not start a new kill every 10 s.
- Owed halt (review 2 M1): until U20b grants the permission K3 always fails, and the runbook's OMS unit is `Restart=on-failure`, `RestartSec=5` (`docs/operations/runbook.md:102-103`; the design wants `Restart=no`, `10_DESIGN_DRAFT.md:549`, U20b). An OMS that crashed and was restarted by systemd reads the marker once, before its boot record (`oms-node/src/node.rs:123-139`), possibly before K1 wrote it; an OMS that only stalled resumes after the loop. Either then answers `/api/status` with no kill latch. So in `Rearming {halt_owed: true}` the first good answer does not arm: `on_answer` returns `OwedHalt` and W5 stays in `Rearming` until the runtime reports the halt's outcome with `owed_halt_done(now, Result<String, String>)`: a reply `"latched"` or starting with `"already latched"` → one `[WATCH P1]` `늦은 정지: OMS가 다시 답해 watcher_kill로 래치함({reply})` and `Armed {now}`; a failure → still `Rearming {halt_owed: true}`, tried again at the next good answer (never two in flight), with one `[WATCH P1]` `늦은 정지 실패: OMS가 답하지만 watcher_kill이 안 됨({why}). OMS가 래치 없이 주문을 낼 수 있음` at the first failure only (and a `해소` line when a later one latches). No new cancel-all loop is started: the latched OMS runs its own (C-KILL). While the halt is owed W5 does not watch for silence (it is `Rearming`); an OMS that dies again before it answers is left to the runbook (known limit). During the kill itself the halt is retried by `Kill`, not by W5 (review 3 M1, below): W5's owed halt is only for a kill that ended (or was aborted) with the halt still owed.
- Marker path on the probe answers (U19b3d; review 3 N6): every good probe answer is also read for `node.kill_marker`; when it fails the marker-path rule (not `configured`, or another resolved path) the runtime sets the lasting P1 condition `marker_path` (repeat rule; text `OMS가 읽는 킬 마커 경로가 워처의 경로와 다름(OMS: {path | 없음}, 워처: {path}). 킬 마커가 OMS를 래치하지 못함: OMS 설정의 [kill] marker를 맞출 것`; clears when an answer passes the rule) and `marker_checked` becomes false while it lasts (so a kill then owes the halt, review 3 S1). It is not a start refusal (the watcher is past its boot) and not a kill.
- `watcher_oms_silence_s` = `(now − last_good_ms) / 1000` while `Armed`; `watcher_w5_armed` = 1 in `Armed` only; `watcher_kill_halt_owed` (gauge 0/1, U19b3c) = 1 in `Rearming {halt_owed: true}` and while a running `Kill` has `halt_owed` (the metric table's reading; review 4 N5).
- Clock (review 4 S3): W5 is given `clock::Steady` (Clock and waits), so a wall clock that steps back does not hold its probes or its silence judgement; `Steady` is built in this part with its unit test.

### The kill sequence (`watcher::kill::Kill`, sans-IO, wraps `KillLoop`, U19b3b)
`Kill::new(fire, cfg, now)` → actions in this order, each step waiting for the previous one's outcome or its deadline (review 2 S1; `Kill::poll(now)` applies the deadlines):
1. **K1 marker**: `WriteMarker {text}`, deadline emission + 2,000 ms (판단(모델 아님)). The runtime runs the write on `tokio::task::spawn_blocking` (never inline: a stalled disk must not stop the tick): `symlink_metadata(marker)`; anything there → `AlreadyPresent` (it is not touched or replaced). Else remove `<dir>/.<file name>.watcher-<pid>.tmp` if it exists (the watcher's own temporary name, never the marker: a watcher killed between create and unlink leaves it, and after a reboot the same pid comes again; `NotFound` is fine; review 3 N2), write `text` to it (created new, mode 0600), `sync_all`, `hard_link` it to the marker path (fails if something appeared meanwhile → `AlreadyPresent`), remove the temporary file, `sync_all` the directory. Any error → `Failed(<io kind>)`; no outcome at the deadline → `Failed("timeout")`, header `쓰지 못함(시간 초과; 뒤늦게 써질 수 있음)`, the late outcome ignored. The sequence goes on in both cases (a missing marker must not delay the stop and the cancel; a hung write must not either). `text` (cut by characters to ≤ 512 bytes so no character is split, review 2 N3: `name` may be 64 characters of up to 4 bytes, `config.rs:241-246`; the OMS reads 512 bytes, `node.rs:487`, `from_utf8_lossy` at `:493-519`): `watcher {name} W5 {UTC RFC 3339 ms}: /api/status gave no good answer for {s} s (last good answer {UTC}); the watcher stops the OMS and cancels every order with its own key. Clear with omsctl clear-hold --reason kill.`
2. **K2 halt**: `Halt {detail}`, deadline emission + 2 × `request_timeout_ms` + 1,000 ms (판단(모델 아님)); past it → not halted (`정지 응답 없음(시간 초과)`) and the runtime aborts the task (review 2 S1) → `oms::admin_kill(path, name, detail, timeout)`: one connection, `{"id":1,"op":"hello","client":"watcher/<name>"}`, then `{"id":2,"op":"watcher_kill","detail":…}`, each reply within `request_timeout_ms`, the whole within 2 × `request_timeout_ms` (as `admin_send`, `oms.rs:128-160`). `detail` = `W5: /api/status gave no good answer for {s} s; watcher {name}` (cut by characters to ≤ 1024 bytes, review 2 N3). Halted = the reply is `"latched"` or starts with `"already latched"` (this covers `KILL_REARMED_REPLY`, `input.rs:122-124`). Anything else (an error code, `bad_request`, no socket, a timeout, a lost hello) → not halted, with the reason.
3. **K3 stop** (only when K2 did not halt): `StopUnit {deadline: now + stop_wait_ms}` → `stop::Stopper::start()` runs exactly `<systemctl> [--user] --no-ask-password stop -- <oms_unit>` with `stdin`/`stdout` null and `stderr` piped, its read end set non-blocking right after `spawn` (`rustix::fs::fcntl_setfl` with `OFlags::NONBLOCK`, `rustix` 1.1.5 feature `fs`, already a dependency; review 3 S6); the runtime calls `try_wait` at every tick; stderr is read only after `try_wait` gave the exit, at most 4 KiB, a `WouldBlock` or end of file ends the read (so a helper that inherited the pipe and keeps it open cannot block the tick; a client that fills the pipe before it exits does not exit and ends at the deadline as `unconfirmed`); exit 0 → stopped; another exit → failed with `exit {code}` and the first line of what was read; the deadline on the injected clock passes → the `systemctl` client is killed (`Child::kill`, SIGKILL) and reaped by `try_wait` at the following ticks, never by a blocking `wait`, outcome `unconfirmed` (review 1 N5: killing the client does not cancel systemd's stop job, so the OMS may still stop later). The header says `정지 확인 못 함({stop_wait_ms} ms 안에 systemctl이 끝나지 않음)`, not `실패`; for the X8 line below an unconfirmed stop counts as a failure (the safe side). `<systemctl>` is `/usr/bin/systemctl` from `Endpoints::of(cfg)`; tests give the path of a fake script under `OMS_TEST_DIR` (no test ever runs the real `systemctl`). `stop.rs` holds the only `std::process::Command::new` of the crate: test `stop::the_watcher_starts_one_program_only` reads `crates/watcher/src/*.rs` at test time and asserts that the text `Command::new` appears once, in `stop.rs` (plain text search).
4. **Loop**: `KillLoop::start(now, header, outcome)` with `header` = `W5: OMS /api/status 무응답 {s}초, 마지막 정상 응답 {KST | 이 프로세스에서 없음}. 킬 마커: {씀 | 씀(OMS가 읽는 경로인지 확인 못 함) | 이미 있음 | 이미 있음(OMS가 읽는 경로인지 확인 못 함) | 쓰지 못함(<kind>)}. OMS 정지: {admin.sock watcher_kill → <reply> | watcher_kill 실패(<why>) → systemctl stop <unit>: 정지됨 | 실패(<why>) | 정지 확인 못 함(…)}`, and, when K2 failed and K3 did not confirm the stop, `. OMS 정지 실패: OMS가 새 주문을 낼 수 있음. OMS 키 폐기(X8 절차)를 검토` (table 7-8 C1 failure column). The first `CancelAll` is emitted only from here: K1 and K2 (and K3 when it runs) have an outcome or passed their deadline before it. This is the done criterion's order.
- **Owed halt** (review 2 M1): `Kill` sets `halt_owed = !halted && !(stopped && marker_in_place)`, where `halted` = K2's reply latched, `stopped` = K3 reported `stopped`, `marker_in_place` = K1 `Written` or `AlreadyPresent` **and** `marker_checked` (review 3 S1: a marker at a path never compared with the OMS's is not in place). It passes the matching `StopOutcome` (`Latched`, `StoppedMarked`, `StoppedUncheckedMarker`, `StoppedUnmarked`, `Unconfirmed`) to `KillLoop::start` for the final notice, and `Report.halt_owed` to the runtime, which calls `W5::kill_ended(halt_owed)`.
- **Halt retried during the loop** (review 3 M1): an OMS that stalled and resumes, or that systemd restarted unlatched during the loop, keeps trading while the watcher's loop cancels its orders, so the loop does not end and an owed halt sent only after `Ended` would never be sent (with a latched or paused watcher key the loop cannot end at all). So while the loop runs and `halt_owed` is true, `Kill::poll` emits `Halt {detail}` again every 5,000 ms (판단(모델 아님)) from the end of the previous attempt, never two in flight, each with the K2 deadline (the runtime runs it as K2, task aborted at the deadline); `detail` = `W5: 늦은 정지: watcher {name}의 킬이 OMS 래치나 정지를 확인하지 못함` (≤ 1024 bytes). A reply that latched sets `halted`, `halt_owed = false`, the `StopOutcome` to `Latched` (the final notice says so), stops the retries and emits one `Notice::LateHalt {reply}` (text `늦은 정지: OMS가 다시 답해 watcher_kill로 래치함({reply cut to 200})`, with the position block like every kill notice). A failed attempt sends nothing (the start notice already says `OMS 정지 실패` or `미확인`) and is retried. The retries need no Bybit request, so they go on when the watcher key is latched or paused. They do not depend on W5 (W5 sends no probe in `Killing`, and silence is still not judged there); an admin connection that is refused costs one failed `connect` per 5 s. A retry still in flight when the loop ends or is aborted (review 4 N1): the runtime aborts its task at `Ended` or at the abort (it does not hold `Ended` for it, and never runs two `watcher_kill` connections at once); a reply that comes later is ignored, and the halt stays owed for W5. If the OMS did latch from that attempt, W5's owed halt then gets `already latched` and sends `늦은 정지`. After `Ended` with the halt still owed, W5's owed halt takes over (above). An aborted kill also reports it (the next watcher start does not know it: known limit, no persistence). Owner decision (default used): a stopped OMS without a marker also owes the halt (safe side: the next start by a person would otherwise run unlatched).
- Free texts in the header (review 1 S9): the K1 io kind, K2's reply and reason, K3's reason are each cut to 200 characters with control characters replaced by spaces (an `admin.sock` error text is bounded only by the 64 KiB line limit, `oms.rs` `LINE_MAX`). The header is then at most about 1,300 UTF-16 units (판단(모델 아님)), so header + body + 20 position rows stay one 3,500-unit part.
- Marker path not yet checked (review 1 S10): `Kill::new` takes `marker_checked: bool` = the runtime saw a status answer in this process that passed the marker-path rule. When false (e.g. W5 fired before any status answer: the watcher started while the OMS was down; or the `marker_path` condition lasts, U19b3d), a written marker shows as `씀(OMS가 읽는 경로인지 확인 못 함)` and an existing one as `이미 있음(OMS가 읽는 경로인지 확인 못 함)`, and it does not count as in place for `halt_owed` (review 3 S1). The marker is written all the same (it is the configured path; refusing to write would lose the latch if the paths agree).
- **`Kill::abort(now, why)`** (review 1 M2), in every step, not only in the loop: before `KillLoop::start` (K1–K3 still running, done or past their deadline) it emits one abort notice whose body is the header built so far, each step not yet ended written `진행 중` (`킬 마커: 진행 중`, `OMS 정지: 진행 중`), then `{워처 종료 | 워처 시작 거부: <reason>}로 워처 킬 중단(일괄 취소 전). OMS가 아직 주문을 낼 수 있음` when no step halted or stopped the OMS, and the fixed position line of the loop's abort notice; then `Ended{aborted}`. A step whose action is running is not cancelled by the abort (a K1 write cannot be, K2's task is aborted, K3's client is left to systemd, as Shutdown below); its outcome is not reported. In the loop, `KillLoop::abort` as U19b2.

The worst time from the last good answer to the first cancel-all (판단(모델 아님), defaults): 10 s silence + 0.1 s tick + 2 s K1 deadline + 5 s K2 deadline (2 × 2 s + 1 s) + 10 s K3 ≈ 27 s; with a local marker and a working `admin.sock` ≈ 10–14 s. The first `[WATCH P1]` of the kill (the start notice) goes out at `KillLoop::start` plus the position read (deadline `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 = 13 s with the defaults, below; review 4 N2): worst ≈ 40 s after the last good answer (review 2 N7; the design asks for P1 once at start and the 12-message bound leaves no room for an earlier one, so C-WATCH part 12 states this worst delay, 판단(모델 아님)).

The OMS, when `watcher_kill` latched it, runs its own kill loop with its own key at the same time (C-KILL). Both are at ≤ 1 cancel-all per second, ≤ 2 per second on the UID together (table 7-8 C4), far below Bybit's 10 per second per UID for cancel-all (Q11.6). If K2 failed and K3 stopped the OMS, only the watcher's loop runs; the marker latches the OMS at its next start (C-HOLD), and that start runs the OMS's loop again before a person can clear.

### Runtime (U19b3c; the bullets marked U19b3d land there)
- From config load: the alert sender (wired in U19b1c) beside the metrics push. From the end of the start (`Done` or `StayUpNoRequests`): W5, in every later phase (status wait, replay, run).
- Queued answers first (review 2 S3): the loop handles one message per turn and then ticks (`runtime.rs:221-227`). From U19b3c, after the message of the turn the runtime also takes every message already in the channel (`try_recv`, at most 1,000 per turn, 판단(모델 아님)) before it ticks, so a probe answer that waits in the channel reaches W5 before `fire`.
- At `Fire`: `watcher_kills_total += 1`, `watcher_kill_active = 1`, one error log line, `Kill::new`. While a kill runs: the engine is not polled (no new rounds, no `watcher_mismatch` send, no heartbeat); answers to its requests in flight are still given to it, but its deadlines run only inside `Engine::poll` (`engine.rs:351-362`), so rounds still open close at the first poll after `Ended` (review 2 N2). The check ages are not frozen: U19b3c moves the age export out of `poll` (`engine.rs:390-395`) into `Engine::export_ages(now)`, which `poll` calls and the runtime calls at every tick during a kill, so `watcher_check_age_s` keeps growing (true: no check runs) instead of standing still; the boot phases go on (they do not call Bybit); `IpCheck` goes on. Reason: the OMS is gone or halted, rounds would fail at their status request anyway, and their Bybit requests would take the guard's room from the loop's counts (판단(모델 아님)). After `Ended`, the engine is polled again (a late round runs once, U19a) and W5 goes to `Rearming {halt_owed}` (`Report.halt_owed`); `watcher_kill_active = 0`.
- Halt retries during the loop (U19b3c; review 3 M1): the runtime runs each `Halt` that `Kill` emits after `KillLoop::start` as it runs K2 (`oms::admin_kill`, the K2 deadline, the task aborted at it), gives the reply to `Kill`, and sends a `LateHalt` notice like the loop's notices (position block, the 16-notice queue; it is never the one dropped).
- Owed halt after the loop (U19b3d; review 2 M1): when W5 returns `OwedHalt`, the runtime runs `oms::admin_kill` with detail `W5: 늦은 정지: watcher {name}의 킬이 OMS 래치나 정지를 확인하지 못함` (≤ 1024 bytes) and the K2 deadline (the task aborted at it), gives the outcome to `owed_halt_done` and sends the P1 W5 returns. The engine is polled meanwhile (no kill runs).
- Consequence of the pause on the OMS's monitoring gate (review 1 S5; the pause is kept, owner question 10): when K2 latched a live OMS, the OMS gets no heartbeat during the loop, so after 30 s (`heartbeat_max_age_us`, `oms-core/src/config.rs:126-133`) it records `hold:unmonitored` and its alert follows, while the watcher runs the kill. Keeping the heartbeat would not avoid it: its W1/W2 times would be older than 10 s and the per-check rule closes the gate as well. The hold blocks only plain orders, which the kill latch already blocks; it clears by itself after the loop, at the first heartbeat whose W1 and W2 succeeded again (up to one W1/W2 round after `Ended`). C-WATCH part 12, runbook 7.16 and 7.18 say so: `모니터링 상실이 워처 킬 중에 따라오고, 루프가 끝나고 W1·W2가 다시 성공하면 스스로 풀린다`.
- Start refusal during a kill (U19b3d; review 1 S10): the marker-path boot rule (the only boot status rule that refuses the start; review 2 N1) when it refuses while a kill runs goes through `Kill::abort(now, "워처 시작 거부: <reason>")` first: the abort notice, then the start-refusal P1 (U19b1c), the alert flush (≤ 5,000 ms), then exit 2. No `tasks.abort_all()` before the notice is queued.
- Owed halt before a marker-path refusal (U19b3d; review 3 S1): when the boot rule refuses while the halt is owed (a kill runs with `halt_owed`, or W5 is in `Rearming {halt_owed: true}`), the answer that fails the rule shows that the OMS answers, so the runtime first runs one `oms::admin_kill` with the owed-halt detail and the K2 deadline (if one is not already in flight: then it waits for that one), logs its outcome and names it in the refusal P1 (`… 늦은 정지: {latched | 실패(<why>)}`), then takes the refusal path above (abort notice if a kill runs, refusal P1, flush, exit 2). A latch is the only thing that keeps an OMS that reads another marker path from trading after the watcher exits.
- Notices (U19b3c) are sent beside the loop, one at a time in their order: for each `Notice` the runtime reads the position block (its own requests, through the guard, never during the key latch or the pause; deadline `request_timeout_ms` + 11,000 ms from the read's start (the engine's one-request bound: the guard's 10 s window plus 1 s, `engine.rs:54-57`, so a read that waited at a full guard behind the loop's count pages is not failed unsent; review 4 N2; at 5,000 ms, review 2 N5, the heaviest kill lost its block), the task aborted at it and the block then `포지션 조회 실패(시간 초과). 수량·손익·마크·강제청산가 모름`; a page handled at or after the deadline counts as past it, and the reader sends no page after it; review 2 N5, review 3 S2), composes the message and calls `alert.once`. A slow position read holds back no `CancelAll` or `Count`. At most 16 notices wait (판단(모델 아님)); beyond it the oldest waiting progress notice is dropped (never a start or final), counted in a log line. Before the end, the final notice is queued before `Ended` is acted on.
- Shutdown (SIGTERM/SIGINT) during a kill (U19b3d): `Kill::abort` (in any step) → the abort notice (no position read, the fixed `포지션 조회 안 함` line) and `Ended`; K3's child, if running, is left to finish its job in systemd (not killed: a half-stopped OMS is worse than a stopped one); then the U19a shutdown steps and the alert flush of up to 5,000 ms. The marker stays. A shutdown during K1 waits for K1's outcome up to its deadline (≤ 2,000 ms), then aborts (review 2 S1). `main.rs` ends the tokio runtime with `shutdown_timeout(1,000 ms)` (판단(모델 아님)) instead of dropping it, because a dropped runtime waits for every `spawn_blocking` task: a write hung on the disk must not hold the exit.
- Nothing is persisted: a watcher restarted during a kill does not resume it. It arms W5 again at the end of its start; an OMS that is still down is killed again (a new marker is not written, the old one is `AlreadyPresent`), and a new loop confirms zero.

### Boot and shutdown summary
Boot: config (with `[w5]`, `[kill]`, `[telegram]`) → credentials and token file → metrics push and alert sender → start checks (with the U19b2a key rules) → W5 armed → wait for `/api/status` (with the marker-path rule) → replay → engine. Shutdown: as U19a, plus: abort a running kill (one notice; during K1 after K1's outcome or deadline), flush alerts ≤ 5 s, end the tokio runtime with `shutdown_timeout(1,000 ms)`, exit 0.

## Testing Decisions

Rules for every part: loopback fakes only (`http-fake` for Bybit, `/api/status`, the IP echo and Telegram; a test-owned `UnixListener` for `admin.sock` and `monitor.sock`; a shell script under `OMS_TEST_DIR` for `systemctl`; an in-process model venue for the engine); journals generated at test time; no real host; no real `systemctl`; a manual clock wherever time matters; every loop in a test on the manual clock has a step bound. Tests that wait for real time (the runtime's 100 ms tick, the sender task, the stopper's child) run 3 times and the part report names each result. A test process killed by signal 9 is unbounded memory growth: find and fix it, never retry it unchanged. W5's gap rule (review 2 S3) judges the time between two runtime ticks. U19a's `walk` moves the manual clock 100 ms per 15 ms of real time (`crates/watcher/tests/runtime.rs:239-244`), so one runtime tick (100 ms real) sees about 670 ms of manual time, and a runtime thread held 600 ms on a loaded gate machine is a 4,000 ms gap (review 3 S4; the earlier text said 100 ms per 100 ms, which was false). So: every runtime test keeps `2 × probe_ms + request_timeout_ms ≥ 4,000` (the defaults: 4,000); every runtime test that waits for `Fire`, for the first cancel-all, for a `watcher_kill` or for `watcher_w5_armed` walks until the condition with a step bound (as `walk_to_recheck`, `tests/runtime.rs:253`), never a fixed length, so a gap that moves `last_good_ms` makes the test later, not red; "no fire before 10 s" is proved in the sans-IO W5 test only; the sans-IO W5 tests poll at least every 100 ms of their clock except where they test a gap.
`walk_kill` (review 4 S2; a helper in `crates/watcher/tests/runtime.rs` beside `walk`): the kill steps K1–K3 and the position read have injected-clock deadlines over real I/O (Clock and waits), so a runtime test does not walk the clock through them. Every runtime test that reaches a kill walks with `walk` only until the instant at which `Fire` is due with no gap (the test knows when its fake status stopped answering: that instant + `silence_ms` + `request_timeout_ms` + 1,000 ms); from there `walk_kill` moves the clock one 100 ms step at a time and after each step waits in real time (bounded, 2,000 ms, 판단(모델 아님)) for the next kill effect the test expects (the marker file, the `watcher_kill` line at the test `admin.sock`, the fake `systemctl`'s argv file, the first `/v5/order/cancel-all` at `http-fake`, the start notice at the fake Telegram); when an effect came, it waits in real time (bounded) until the runtime has acted on its outcome (the next effect, or the K2 reply written and one tick passed) before it moves the clock again. So a K step gets at most one 100 ms step of manual time while it runs, far inside its deadline (2,000 ms, 5,000 ms, `stop_wait_ms`, 13,000 ms), whatever the machine's `fsync` takes; the step count is bounded (판단(모델 아님): 300 steps) and the test fails with the name of the effect it waited for. This applies to `runtime::oms_silence_writes_the_marker_and_halts_the_oms_before_the_first_cancel_all`, `runtime::when_watcher_kill_fails_the_oms_unit_is_stopped_before_the_first_cancel_all`, `runtime::a_marker_written_by_the_watcher_is_read_by_a_marker_rule`, `runtime::a_watcher_started_with_the_oms_down_kills_after_the_silence` and every other runtime test that asserts a K1, K2 or K3 outcome or a start-notice header. A test that steps faster than `walk` (the 600 s test) does so only after the start notice reached the fake Telegram (the 600 s window is counted from `s`, so nothing is lost; not after `Fire`, which would give K1 about 30 ms of real time), and asserts message counts only, not the position block's text (a progress notice's position read may then time out on the fast clock; the block's text is tested at `walk_kill` pace in `runtime::every_kill_notice_carries_the_position_block`).
Guards against touching this PC or a real host by mistake (review 3 N5): runtime and stopper tests use the unit name `watcher-test-no-such-unit.service`; the test helper that builds `Endpoints` refuses a `systemctl` path outside `OMS_TEST_DIR` (panics); the pinned-route tests resolve the reserved name `telegram.invalid` to the fake (`reqwest` `resolve`), never `api.telegram.org`, so a missing `resolve` fails to resolve instead of reaching Telegram.

### Known limits written in C-WATCH (U19b3a–U19b3d unless noted)
- W5 sees a dead process or a silent HTTP task only: a hung core or pipeline behind an answering `/api/status` is not a W5 kill (review 1 S1; owner question 11).
- During a watcher kill of a live OMS, the OMS's `hold:unmonitored` and its alert follow after 30 s and clear after the loop (review 1 S5).
- A watcher started before the OMS answers, or with a wrong `status_url`, kills the OMS after `silence_ms` (owner question 1; runbook start order).
- Time the watcher did not observe (its own stall, a clock step forward) is not counted as OMS silence (gap rule, review 2 S3); a real silence that overlaps it is counted from the gap's end, so W5 fires up to one gap later; a watcher that stalls again and again (each gap > `2 × probe_ms + request_timeout_ms`) does not fire W5 at all, and the OMS's own monitoring gate (no heartbeat for 30 s → `hold:unmonitored`) is then the only guard.
- While the halt is owed, `Kill` retries `watcher_kill` every 5,000 ms during the loop and W5 sends it at the OMS's first good answer after the loop (review 2 M1, review 3 M1). An OMS that comes back unlatched trades until the next attempt reaches it: up to 5 s plus one K2 deadline during the loop, and after the loop up to one probe interval (its own boot time aside). While the halt is owed after the loop W5 does not watch for silence. The owed halt is in memory: a watcher restart forgets it.
- The watcher's loop ends only at a confirmed zero or a watcher stop; an operator who clears the kill hold while it runs lets it cancel a trading OMS's orders (review 2 S5; runbook 7.18).
- A marker write that does not return within 2,000 ms is given up (`쓰지 못함(시간 초과; 뒤늦게 써질 수 있음)`); the write may still land later (review 2 S1).
- The first `[WATCH P1]` of a kill goes out up to ≈ 40 s after the last good answer with the defaults (판단(모델 아님); review 2 N7, review 4 N2: the position read may wait up to 13 s at the request guard).
- The marker race of U18b1 N4: an operator's `clear_hold kill` whose unlink comes right after the watcher wrote a new marker removes it; the watcher's start and final notices say whether the marker was present when it looked (`킬 마커: …`), and the OMS-side fix (unlink only the inode seen at boot) is a later OMS unit (Out of Scope).
- A kill confirms zero for linear/USDT only (U19b2a).
- The `stop` needs a systemd permission that U20b grants; until then K3 fails, the start notice says `OMS 정지 실패` (the loop still runs), the final notice says `OMS 정지 미확인` and the halt is owed (review 2 M1). A `systemctl` client that does not end within `stop_wait_ms` is killed, but systemd's stop job may still stop the OMS later (`정지 확인 못 함`).
- No persistence: a watcher restart forgets a running kill; W5 kills again if the OMS is still down after the start.
- The 12-message bound counts the loop's kill notices; the lasting conditions (`auth`, `public_ip`, `marker_path`) add ≤ 1 + ⌊T / 5 min⌋ each (U19b1c, U19b3d); the owed halt adds ≤ 1 during the loop (`LateHalt`, review 3 M1) or ≤ 2 after it (`늦은 정지 실패` once, then `늦은 정지` or nothing; review 2 M1), never both (a latch during the loop ends the debt).
- Without `[kill]` (testnet only) W5 is off: the watcher neither stops the OMS nor tells that it is silent; the OMS's own monitoring gate and the observability stack are then the only signals (review 3 N7).
- The marker path is compared at boot (a refusal) and on W5's probe answers (`marker_path` P1, review 3 N6); an OMS restarted with another path between two probes is seen at the next probe.
- A wall clock that steps back by Δ (review 4 S3): W5 and the kill path read `clock::Steady` and go on as if no time passed at the step; the sender's retry pace (5 s after a flush that left parts), the repeat rule and the mismatch notice pace read the raw clock and wait up to Δ (a new message is still sent at once). Accepted: the P1 that matters most (the kill's start notice) is a new message.
- While the start checks retry (a start request without a usable answer is asked again every 5,000 ms, `start.rs:30-32`, or after 600 s on HTTP 403), W5 is not armed and no P1 says that the watcher is stuck in its start; an OMS that dies then is told by nobody on the watcher's side (the OMS's own monitoring gate and the observability stack are the signals; `watcher_w5_armed = 0` is the series a U20b rule can watch; review 4 N10).
- A start refusal after the sender is up sends one `[WATCH P1]` per start; a unit that restarts the watcher on exit 2 would send one per restart. The ops doc says the watcher unit must not restart on exit 2 (`RestartPreventExitStatus=2`, as the OMS unit does for 3, `runbook.md:104-105`; U20b writes the unit; review 4 N9).

## Owner questions (the parts use the default; the runner does not wait)

1. **When W5 starts watching.** Default: at the end of the watcher's start checks, whether or not the OMS ever answered in this process; a watcher started while the OMS is down kills 10 s later (orders left by a crashed OMS get cancelled; the cost is a kill and a `clear-hold` when the watcher is started first by mistake). Alternative: only after the first good answer (no kill at a cold start, but a crashed OMS's orders stay if the watcher restarted after the crash).
2. **Kill notices with an unchanged count.** Default: one progress notice every 60 s while the loop runs, also when the count did not change (table 7-8 "바뀌지 않아도 60초마다"); a changed count waits for the next 60 s mark (the scope's "at most once per 60 s"). Alternative A: a notice only when the count changed (quiet while orders stay stuck). Alternative B: the OMS's rule (`kill.rs:192-235`): a change notice at once for every changed known count above zero, plus the 60 s repeat; it does not keep the 12-in-10-min bound when the count changes often (review 1 S4).
3. **Count after a failed cancel-all** (unlike the OMS). Default: yes, the watcher counts after every sent cancel-all, so an undocumented error answer for "nothing to cancel" cannot keep the loop from ending. Alternative: the OMS's rule.
4. **How the watcher may stop the OMS unit.** Default: `systemctl --no-ask-password stop -- <oms_unit>` on the system manager (`[kill] systemd = "system"`), with the permission for that one unit given by U20b; a failed stop is told in the start notice and the loop still runs. Alternative: both as user units of one user (`systemd = "user"`).
5. **Key permissions checked at start when `[kill]` is set.** Default: refuse a read-only key, a key without `ContractTrade: Order`, and a key with any `Withdraw`. Alternative: only log.
6. **Telegram bot and file.** Default: the same bot as the OMS (Q18); the watcher reads its own `token_file`, which may be the OMS's file (same format). Alternative: a separate bot.
7. **Mismatch P1 pace.** Default: one per confirmation, at most one message per check per 60 s, the rest collected into the next. Alternative: every confirmation at once.
8. **SIGTERM during a kill.** Default: the loop stops at once with one notice; the marker and the OMS's latch stay. Alternative: finish the loop before exiting (a person who stops the watcher then waits up to the loop's end).
9. **Testnet without `[kill]` or `[telegram]`.** Default: allowed with one `warn` each (W5 off, P1 only logged), as the OMS's optional sections on testnet; mainnet refuses both. Cost: on such a testnet watcher an OMS that goes silent sends no `[WATCH P1]` (review 3 N7). Alternative: refuse on testnet too, or run W5 alert-only there.
10. **The engine during a kill.** Default: paused (no checks, no heartbeat, no `watcher_mismatch`), so the loop has the request guard to itself. Cost of the default: when `watcher_kill` latched a live OMS, its monitoring gate goes `unmonitored` about 30 s into the loop (`모니터링 상실` alert) and clears after the loop (review 1 S5); keeping only the heartbeat would not avoid it (its W1/W2 times would age past 10 s). Alternative: keep W1–W4 running.
11. **A hung OMS core behind an answering `/api/status`** (review 1 S1). Default: not a W5 kill; W5 watches `/api/status` only (design §7.5), and the limit is written down (C-WATCH part 12, V-WATCH-13 `not_covered`). Alternative: a `monitor.sock` heartbeat that gets no `recorded` for N s (N 판단(모델 아님), e.g. 30 s) also counts as OMS loss and starts the kill.
12. **Time the watcher did not observe** (review 2 S3). Default: not OMS silence (the gap rule: a gap of more than `2 × probe_ms + request_timeout_ms` between two W5 polls moves `last_good_ms` forward), and W5 fires only after a failed probe; so a watcher that was itself stopped or starved does not kill a healthy OMS. Cost: a real OMS death during a watcher stall is seen up to one gap later. Alternative: the U19a-style rule (any 10 s without a good answer at a tick kills; the safe side for orders, a kill and a `clear-hold` for a person after every watcher stall).
13. **A kill that did not confirm a latch or a stop** (review 2 M1). Default: the halt is owed; W5 sends `watcher_kill` at the OMS's next good answer and tells it (`늦은 정지` / `늦은 정지 실패`); a stopped OMS without a marker, or with a marker at a path never checked against the OMS's (review 3 S1), owes it too; during the loop the halt is retried every 5,000 ms (review 3 M1). Alternative: no late halt (the OMS that came back trades unlatched until a person acts on the `OMS 정지 미확인` line). Related, for U20b: the runbook's OMS unit is `Restart=on-failure` (`runbook.md:102-103`), the design's is `Restart=no` (`10_DESIGN_DRAFT.md:549`); the owed halt is needed with either, because a stalled OMS resumes without a restart.
